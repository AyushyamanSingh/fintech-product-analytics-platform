# Case study - FinTech Product Analytics & Decision Intelligence Platform

_Ayushyaman Singh · Data Analyst (product analytics, BI) · personal project · **all data synthetic**: Vittora Credit is a
fictional lender and every record was generated by a seeded simulator._

---

## Business problem

Vittora Credit, a digital lender with seven products (personal loans, salary advance, BNPL, consumer durables,
business micro-loans, a P2P marketplace and a new flexi credit line), was growing fast - ~4,250 new customers in
June 2026, AUM ₹29.56 Cr - but leadership could not answer five questions with confidence:

1. Where exactly does the onboarding funnel leak, and for whom?
2. Which acquisition channels create customers worth more than they cost?
3. Is credit quality holding up after the festive-season push?
4. Did the onboarding redesign work - and was it safe to ship?
5. Can we trust the numbers? (Every team reported from its own spreadsheet; event data and backend data disagreed.)

Underneath sat an operational problem: the weekly business review took days of manual assembly and still produced
conflicting figures.

## Data

A seeded simulator generated 24 months (Jul 2024 - Jun 2026) of eleven source-system tables - **1,252,160 rows**:

| System | Tables | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| CRM | customers | 81,525 |
| Loan origination | applications | 46,988 |
| Loan management | loans, repayments | 20,661 + 125,584 |
| Payment gateway | transactions | 131,285 |
| Mixpanel export | product_events (16 event types) | 832,231 |
| Helpdesk, experimentation, marketing, product config | tickets, experiments, assignments, campaigns, products | 13,886 |

Two things make the dataset useful for proving analytical methods rather than just demonstrating charts:

* **Planted business truths** - KYC friction by platform and segment, channel quality differences, a festive-season
  credit dilution, a payment-gateway outage, an app-release tracking bug, an affiliate fraud ring, three experiments.
* **Planted data defects** - 27 types (duplicates, timezone errors, unit errors, orphans, schema and type drift ...)
  recorded in an answer key, so the data-quality framework's recall can be measured.

## Approach

1. **Trust first.** Wrote data contracts (YAML) and a check library; profiled the raw extract; designed repair rules
   only for defects with a known, reversible root cause; quarantined the rest; made the curated layer a publish gate.
2. **Model once.** Loaded a DuckDB warehouse with enforced keys and built eight marts so every business definition
   (funnel stage, revenue, DPD, active user) exists in exactly one place.
3. **Answer the questions.** 50 SQL analyses, a 28-KPI governed layer, an experimentation module, segmentation and
   anomaly detection - each tied to a stakeholder question.
4. **Make it repeatable.** One orchestrated pipeline, a weekly schedule, tests in CI, and an AI analyst that turns the
   results into a stakeholder summary without being allowed to invent numbers.

## Architecture

`source CSVs → extract (lineage) → DQ checks (raw) → transform (repair · quarantine · log) → DQ gate (curated) →
DuckDB core + marts → SQL analytics · KPIs · experiments · segments · anomalies → fact sheet → Claude summary with
numeric verification · Power BI star schema`. Diagrams: [architecture.md](architecture.md).

## Analytics

| Question | Method | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Where does the funnel leak? | Customer-level funnel with 7/14/30-day windows and maturity flags; drop-off by segment with GROUPING SETS and "recoverable customers" | `sql/analytics/01-02` |
| Do customers stay? | Monthly-active cohort matrix; borrower vs non-borrower curves; MAU growth accounting (new / retained / resurrected / churned, quick ratio) | `03-04` |
| What is a customer worth? | Realised net value (revenue - losses, NPA treated as lost) for 12-month+ cohorts vs allocated CAC; cumulative value curves; value concentration by decile | `05`, `13` |
| How healthy is the book? | Point-in-time month-end PAR30/NPA from instalment history; vintage curves by months-on-book; FPD30 by segment | `07` |
| Do good customers come back? | Repeat within 90 days of closure; time to next loan; cross-sell matrix (LEAD) | `09` |
| What happened, and why? | Seasonality-aware robust anomaly detection with driver attribution; event-vs-backend reconciliation | `12`, `15`, `python/anomaly_detection.py` |

## Key findings

1. **KYC is the leak.** 59.0% of signups complete KYC; 21,205 customers are lost between starting and finishing
   KYC - the largest loss at any step. Web completes at 43.9% within 7 days versus 60.9% on iOS (-17.0 pp); tier-3,
   gig workers, students and new-to-credit applicants also lag.
2. **Cheap signups are expensive borrowers.** Paid social and affiliate convert signups to borrowers at ~8% (vs 34%
   for employer partnerships) and return **1.3x-1.4x** their acquisition cost; referral returns **9.4x**,
   partnerships **9.0x**, paid search 2.5x.
3. **Festive growth borrowed from the future.** First-loan FPD30 for affiliate loans disbursed in Oct-Nov 2025 was
   4.49% vs 1.71% in the preceding months; those loans are now being written off - June 2026 credit losses rose
   **46.1% month-on-month** and net revenue fell **17.2%** while gross revenue still grew 1.6%.
4. **Fraud shows up in four places at once.** The "Affiliate Network B" push (8-21 Sep 2025) produced a 14-day
   signup spike (peak 222 vs 113 expected), an 11.7 pp drop in the month's approval rate, KYC-mismatch and fraud
   rejections at 43% of all rejections, and 4 funded customers for ₹5.56 L of spend (**₹1.39 L per funded customer**).
5. **Not every anomaly is a business problem.** In May 2025 tracked KYC completions fell to 33.9% of the backend count
   on the worst day - an Android 5.2.0 release had dropped the `kyc_completed` event (52.5% of completions on that build
   were tracked). Backend KYC was normal. The 14-15 Aug 2025 payment-failure spike (47.1% vs 8.9% expected, 96% gateway
   timeouts) *was* real.
6. **Value is concentrated and product mix matters.** The top 10% of borrowers generate 59.4% of net revenue.
   K-Means on 11 behavioural and value features (k = 6; silhouette is low for every k, so k was chosen for
   actionability and checked for stability - adjusted Rand index 0.999 across seeds) finds *high-value borrowers*
   (24.9% of borrowers, 69.2% of net revenue), *loyal repeat borrowers* (8.6%, 18.7%; ~2.9 loans each), *new &
   ramping* (26.4%, 13.7%), *chronic late payers* (10.7%, 10.3%; one-third of instalments on time), *lapsed one-time
   borrowers* (27.4%, 8.9%) and *credit-stressed* (2.0%, -20.8%). On the CRM side, the "high-value repeat" rule
   segment is 1.6% of customers and 33.4% of net revenue. Personal loans are 53.3% of revenue; BNPL is 24% of loans
   but 1.3% of revenue - an acquisition product, not a profit product.
7. **Borrowing is what retains users.** Borrowers are still active at month 3 at 74.1% (39.1% at month 12); customers
   who never borrow fall to 7.8% by month 3.
8. **Autopay is the repayment lever.** Autopay borrowers return for a repeat loan within 90 days 56.4% of the time vs
   46.9% without (correlational); the autopay-nudge experiment raised take-up by 17.7 pp (causal).

## Experiment

**EXP-ONB-2026-01 - onboarding redesign** (trust signals: regulated-lender badge, encryption note, DigiLocker one-tap;
plus a 3-step progress bar). Hypothesis came from the funnel analysis: KYC friction is a trust and effort problem.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Design | 50/50 randomisation of new app signups, 6 weeks (12 Jan - 22 Feb 2026), primary metric KYC completed within 7 days |
| Power | pre-period baseline 57.4%, MDE 4 pp, alpha 0.05, 80% power → ~2,400 users per arm → 6 weeks at ~800 app signups/week |
| Validity | control 2,543 vs treatment 2,407; SRM chi-square p ≈ 0.05 (alarm threshold 0.001) - no mismatch |
| Primary result | 58.51% → 61.78%: **+3.26 pp (95% CI +0.54 to +5.99), +5.6% relative, p = 0.019** |
| Secondary | application within 14 days +1.46 pp (n.s.); first loan within 30 days +2.15 pp (p = 0.041) |
| Guardrails (non-inferiority) | approval rate +3.13 pp (pass, margin 2 pp); FPD30 -1.48 pp (pass, margin 1.5 pp); KYC support tickets +0.16 pp (inconclusive - CI upper bound 1.19 pp vs 1.0 pp margin) |
| Decision | **Ship, with post-launch monitoring of KYC support tickets.** Rolled out 2 Mar 2026; 7-day KYC completion for June 2026 is 62.3% (+6.0 pp year on year) |
| Estimated impact | ~1,500 more verified customers and ~390 more first loans a year at the current run rate, ≈ ₹16.6 L net revenue from first loans alone (repeat-loan value excluded) |

What I would flag to stakeholders: the observed lift is below the 4 pp the test was sized for, so the interval is wide
(+0.5 to +6.0 pp) - the decision is sound, the exact size is uncertain, which is why the weekly KYC trend is tracked
after launch. Two other tests are documented the same way: the autopay nudge (+17.7 pp autopay take-up, shipped) and
fee transparency (+2.2 pp, not significant; with 410 users per arm the test could not detect the hypothesised 3 pp -
reported as underpowered, not as "no effect").

## AI automation

The weekly business review is written automatically:

1. The pipeline compiles a **fact sheet** of 73 numbered facts (KPIs with deltas and targets, anomaly episodes,
   experiment results, segments, data-quality status), each with its source file.
2. **Claude** (Opus 5.5, structured JSON output) writes the headline, highlights, KPI movements, anomalies,
   experiments, risks, owned recommendations and data caveats, citing fact ids for every item.
3. A **grounding checker** extracts every number - rupees, lakh/crore, %, pp - and requires it to appear in a fact the
   item cites. Failing items get a targeted retry; anything still unverified is removed and the removal is reported.
4. Natural-language **Q&A** works through tools: the model writes SQL, a guard allows only single SELECT statements on
   allow-listed tables over a read-only connection, and every number in the answer must match the returned rows.

Without an API key a deterministic template produces the same structure from the same facts and passes the same
checker (the current sample verifies 116 numbers across 37 items). The checker also caught a bug in my own template
- a "D-3, D-1" reminder schedule read as an unverified number - which is the point: it checks *any* text, not just the
model's.

## Technical challenges

| Challenge | What I did |
|---|---|
| pandas silently turned `"N/A"` into NaN on read, hiding a numeric-column type drift from the checks | Extract reads values as delivered (`keep_default_na=False`); typing happens in one governed place |
| Free-text channel labels contradicted campaign tags (attribution conflict) | Campaign tag is the source of truth; the ETL derives the channel and a reconciliation check counts conflicts |
| Month-end PAR30/NPA must not be rewritten by later payments | Rebuilt month-end DPD from instalment history (point-in-time snapshot mart) |
| Anomaly detector first raised 64 episodes - mostly noise on small-denominator days and post-campaign "drops" | Binomial/Poisson noise floors, minimum trials, campaign days excluded from baselines, persistence rule → 8 episodes, all explainable |
| The detector flagged a DAU surge in the last 10 days of data | It was my generator: recent users' expected logins were squeezed into the remaining days (right-censoring). Fixed with exposure-scaled rates - monitoring caught a bug in the data producer |
| An LLM will produce plausible numbers | Fact sheet + citations + numeric checker + SQL guard + deterministic fallback |
| Python 3.8 environment, slow network, no pyarrow | Parquet through DuckDB; new API parameters passed via the SDK's `extra_body`; pinned wheels |
| Experiment secondary/guardrail metrics are underpowered by design | Non-inferiority margins with an explicit "inconclusive" state that becomes a monitoring item |

## Results

What the platform delivers (on synthetic data):

* **One command, ~2 minutes**: regenerate sources → validated warehouse → 50 analyses → KPIs → experiments →
  segments → anomalies → charts → Power BI extract → verified executive summary.
* **Data trust made measurable**: DQ score 92.0 → 99.1, critical failures 22 → 0, **27/27** planted defect types
  detected, 305 unrepairable rows quarantined with reasons, every repair logged.
* **Every planted business pattern recovered** by the analytics, and 8 anomaly episodes that each map to a real event.
* **Experiment discipline**: three documented readouts with design, validity, inference, guardrails and decisions.
* **Automated weekly review** whose numbers are 100% traceable to source files; runs without an LLM if needed.
* **77 automated tests** (generator invariants, every DQ check, ETL rules, statistics against textbook values,
  detector behaviour, grounding and SQL-guard safety, contract/DDL consistency, full end-to-end run) in CI.

## What I learned

* **Definitions are the product.** Most disagreements between teams are definition disagreements; a governed KPI
  registry shared by SQL, BI and AI removed the argument before it started.
* **Data quality needs an answer key.** Planting defects and measuring recall turned "we have checks" into "our checks
  catch 27 of 27 known failure modes" - and exposed two silent gaps I would not have found otherwise.
* **Separate data incidents from business incidents.** The same chart (KYC completions dropping) meant "nothing
  happened" in May 2025 and "customers are failing" elsewhere; reconciliation against the system of record is what
  tells them apart.
* **Report experiments honestly.** A significant result below the planned MDE still deserves its wide interval, and an
  underpowered null is "we can't tell", not "no effect".
* **LLMs are best at language, not arithmetic.** Keeping every calculation in SQL/Python and making the model cite
  verified facts gave fast, readable summaries without the risk of a confident wrong number in a board deck.
